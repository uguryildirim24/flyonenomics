# ADHD in a fly: evidence and a map onto the male-brain model

> **Current status (25 September 2026):** Historical research review checked 22 September 2026, before adoption of the MaleCNS substrate `rest:ed9b0a469d7a6b77` and filled male drive/dopamine tables. See [adoption](malecns-port.md#starting-substrate-port-step-6-item-155), the [first-study design](adhd-study-design.md), the [confirmation design](adhd-confirm-design.md) and the [parameter/engine provenance erratum](errata.md). The affine-controlled contrast recommended here became a **secondary** in the frozen first-study design; its numerical fit later became unstable and cannot resolve a non-affine effect ([results](adhd-study-results.md#key-secondary-affine-residual--retained-but-unstable)).

## In plain language

**Flies can model particular features relevant to ADHD, not the human diagnosis.** The strongest practical starting point here is **fumin**, a mutation that disables the dopamine transporter—the protein that clears released dopamine. Real fumin flies stay active longer and sleep less; that does not establish an attention deficit. Importantly, their original study found more activity over the day, **not faster activity whenever awake**. [1]

For Rolf's first experiment, I recommend:

- **Change:** disable modelled dopamine uptake, using the existing fumin manipulation.
- **Measure:** whether the recent history of two competing inputs changes their relative representation in the ring neurons, and whether transporter loss changes that history effect. This is a neural measurement, not a simulated fly choosing where to look.
- **Intervene:** reduce dopamine production/release with a **3-iodotyrosine-like** manipulation. Adult fly-brain experiments support the direction of this biochemical effect. They do not establish that it rescues fumin attention. [10]
- **Do not use methylphenidate as the primary rescue of this knockout.** Its only implemented action requires the transporter that fumin removes. A zero drug effect would follow from the code, not discover treatment resistance in flies.

Item 146's two-input, controlled-history experiment is the right starting framework. It must be rebuilt and frozen for **MaleCNS v1.0**, not copied as a validated result from FlyWire. The model has no body or demonstrated vision; its inputs are injected downstream of the eye. The defensible project is **a reproducible test of how an ADHD-relevant dopamine perturbation changes neural representation**, not “we replicated ADHD.”

Several numbers and citations in `docs/research/L2-biology.md` are incorrect or unsupported. In particular, the cited atomoxetine study found **increased**, not decreased, activity; the candidate-gene studies are not the screen described there; and methylphenidate did not rescue every attention-like measure in radish flies. The corrections below replace those claims for planning this experiment.

**Scope and provenance.** Research and code inspection only; no simulations, box jobs or code changes. Checked 2026-09-22 against repository commit `18072403d27c4951f1444d62a4792fe549eaf2f3`. File:line references refer to that snapshot. Read `HANDOFF.md`, `docs/brief.md`, L2, SPEC-P2 §2.5 and §10 items 127–155. Items 149–155 supersede the older handoff's stopped-project status for this task. At that snapshot, the male dopamine/drive calibration had not yet been adopted into the runtime tables; the subsequent adoption is documented in the port. ## 1. What to retain and correct from L2

This is a focused audit of the evidence needed for the first experiment, not a replacement review of every fly dopamine paper. **FT** below means the relevant full-text methods/results were checked (PMC HTML or Europe PMC XML); **A** means abstract only. Numerical results are published values, not graph estimates. “Not extracted” does not mean the paper has no quantitative data.

1. **Keep fumin as a dopamine-clearance/arousal model; change the phenotype description.** Kume et al. report total daily activity at **two to three times** control, with a similar activity index during active periods. They report no significant rebound in their deprivation experiments, not proof that every form of sleep homeostasis is absent. L2's exact sleep-minute comparison and its attribution of a 3-IY rescue to Kume 2005 are not established by that paper. [1]
2. **Keep the DAT/Cirl/Nf1 result, but distinguish RNAi from a null.** Van der Voet et al. experimentally tested three selected genes plus controls, alongside database enrichment; they did not perform the later broad candidate screen implied by L2. They measured beam crossings and inactivity-defined sleep, not walking velocity, visual attention or impulsivity. Their reported 10-fold DAT and sixfold Cirl activity figures compare **nighttime versus daytime mutant-minus-control differences**, not mutant/control activity ratios. MPH was **1 mg/mL food**, not the 0.1–1 mM range L2 attributes to this study. Cirl represents the fly latrophilin family, not a uniquely one-to-one human ADGRL3 copy. [2]
3. **Replace L2's “35% of lines / FBXO25, ANK3, GPM6A, ST3GAL3 knockdown” account.** Rohde et al. tested 14 candidate-gene disruptions, with aberrant activity in all but one; this does not mean 13 hyperactive lines. [3, A] Klein et al. tested fly **dMEF2 and dTRAPPC9**, not ST3GAL3, functionally. [4, A] Harich et al.'s FBXO25 experiment used **overexpression**, not knockdown. [5] The other Harich DOI cited by L2 is a human CNV prioritization study, not that fly screen. [19, A] These sources do not substantiate L2's combined list, habituation claim or hit fraction.
4. **Keep radish as attention-like evidence, but narrow the rescue.** MPH improved optomotor performance and novelty-linked neural selection/suppression; it **did not significantly correct ongoing random alternation dynamics**. The closed-loop flight result and the drug-tested maze/LFP results are distinct assays. L2's distractor-switch percentages, odds ratio and generic Cohen's d ranges are not substantiated by this paper and should not be reused. [6]
5. **Correct the frequency-tagging example.** Grabowska et al. used tethered **walking on a ball**, **5.9/6.6 Hz** tags and activation of **dNPF-expressing reward circuitry**, not 12/15 Hz tags and direct dopamine-neuron activation. [7] Cohen et al. 2016 studied **isoflurane anesthesia**, with 13/17 Hz tags, not the claimed attention experiment. [20, A] Do not inherit L2's SSVEP gain ratios or its proposed LFP-from-spikes equivalence.
6. **Correct atomoxetine and the exposure duration.** Qu et al. selected wild-type male flies showing **hyperactivity after either MPH or ATX**, following **24 h** exposure. The chosen food concentrations were 1.5 mg/mL MPH and 0.25 mg/mL ATX. This is not evidence for an ATX-calming profile or a 12 h receptor-recovery law. Their transcriptomics does not measure receptor protein turnover, dopamine concentration or transporter inhibition. [18]
7. **Correct the synthesis/receptor evidence.** Kim et al.'s learning paper has DOI **10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1167-07.2007**, not L2's 1764-07 identifier. [13, A] In Saldes et al., Dop1R1 loss **prevented caffeine-induced impulsivity**; it did not demonstrate impaired inhibitory control caused by Dop1R1 loss. MB α/β as well as γ cells were implicated. [15]
8. **Separate development from an adult dopamine knob.** Ferguson et al. found lasting adult responsiveness defects after pupal dopamine activation or D1-like receptor knockdown, localizing an effect to a restricted dFB driver. They did **not** establish a measured 40% pruning intervention or permanent dFB arborization loss to transplant into this graph. Gross dopamine circuitry was reported as unaffected in the earlier work they discuss; altered connectivity is a proposed explanation. This was framed as a dopamine-ontogeny/schizophrenia study, not a direct ADHD genotype study. [14]
9. **Retain uptake kinetics with their actual scope.** The 0.11 ± 0.02 μM/s Vmax and 1.3 ± 0.6 μM Km are from **Vickrey, Xiao and Venton, larval CNS**, corrected for diffusion—not Pyakurel or a measured adult male EB compartment. [9, A] Shin and Venton measured adult ex vivo dopamine release/clearance; they do not supply L2's universal 1–5 μm diffusion distance or subsecond clearance rule. [10]
10. **Do not reuse unsupported quantitative priors.** L2's remaining assay-wide effect-size ranges, food-to-brain “10–100×” conversion, universal receptor recovery times and specified desensitization/sensitization schedules were not verified here. They are excluded from this recommendation, not silently treated as observations. Concrete inherited problems remain in `data/params-v0.2.yaml:85` (Kume/3-IY attribution), `:88` (L2 food-to-brain claim) and `:94` (MPH dose-source attribution). The frozen files remain unchanged; their numerical values are declared model assumptions, not verified fly pharmacology. See the [provenance erratum](errata.md) for corrected source descriptions.

**Where the findings disagree or require separation.**

- “More dopamine means better attention” is not a literature result. DA manipulations can reduce or increase activity, and effects differ by assay, life stage, dose and developmental timing. Adult neural DA deficiency preserved some visual functions [12, A]; both reduced and elevated signaling disrupted other visual-response measures [11, A]. Receptor effects also differ across ring populations [16]. These are not grounds to fit a desired inverted-U curve.
- MPH calmed the adult RNAi models in [2], stimulated larval movement in [17], increased wild-type adult movement in [18], and rescued only some radish measurements in [6]. Controls, exposure durations and endpoints differ. The studies establish **context dependence**, not a contradiction resolvable by one universal DAT-to-activity sign.
- DAT RNAi rescue by MPH [2] and absence of an MPH locomotor increase in fumin larvae [17] concern different degrees of transporter disruption and different stages/assays. Residual transporter, other drug targets and compensatory biology are possibilities, not mechanisms established by those contrasts.
- Hyperactivity is neither necessary nor sufficient for the specific inhibitory-control deficit in [15]. Candidate-gene activity screens [2–5] are therefore not attention or impulsivity screens. Lack of the locomotor signature also does not rule out an ADHD contribution; for example, the GIT1 replication study found morphological effects without a fly locomotor effect and did not confirm the human genetic association. [21, A]

## 3. Map onto the actual implementation

### What the model already means

The male registry contains **282 ER-labelled ring cells**, with **245** in the anatomy-selected directly-TuBu-reached trace set, and **27 CX_DAN** cells. These are model populations, not identities of experimental GAL4 lines (`data/populations-male-cns-v1.0.yaml:85,859,964`; `scripts/build_malecns_populations.py:256–266`; SPEC-P2 item 152). The `dFB` population is **140 FB6/FB7-labelled cells**, not Ferguson's approximately 20 driver-labelled cells (`data/populations-male-cns-v1.0.yaml:948`).

Dopamine has 37 pools: bilateral MB compartments plus EB, PB, FB, NO and LAL (`data/compartments-male-cns-v1.0.yaml:2–47`). Innervation **M** maps neural events into pools, exposure **W** maps pools back onto neurons (`src/flyonenomics/registry/__init__.py:231–243,388–398`). Pool release and uptake are in `src/flyonenomics/neuromod/pools.py:39–53`:

- release per chunk = `rel × (alpha_c × weighted DAN counts + S_c × dt)`;
- clearance rate = `Vmax × DA/(Km + DA) + k_ns × DA`.

Only two active receptor channels affect cells: Dop1R1-like `r1` and Dop2R-like `r2`. They change **spike threshold and incoming-event gain**, not outgoing synaptic weights or learning (`src/flyonenomics/neuromod/receptors.py:11–20`; `state.py:301–327`). Gain multiplies **negative and positive** incoming weights (`src/flyonenomics/engine/models.py:15–25`), so increasing gain need not increase net excitation. `rq`/Dop1R2 is census-only for dynamics; DopEcR is absent.

Receptor densities are explicitly placeholders (`data/receptors-male-cns-v1.0.yaml:3,23–52,78–107`). All ER cells share `r1=1, r2=0.5`; this is not a measured male subtype map. Occupancy affinities, threshold/gain coefficients, pharmacokinetics and 3-IY potency are also placeholders (`data/params-v0.2.yaml:70–94`). The larval uptake measurements [9] support their source, not adult-compartment validity.

**Runtime readiness at the reviewed snapshot:** `data/dopamine-male-cns-v1.0.yaml:43–194` then had null R/alpha/source/mode arrays and `:203` said calibration unset; the loader rejected these (`src/flyonenomics/drive/rest.py:116–131`). The resting configuration was subsequently selected and adopted as a distinct male substrate. This did not make old FlyWire experiment files male-compatible. It was a dependency for the first study, not a reason to run anything at the review stage.

## 4. Recommendation and its claim ceiling

### Primary manipulation: modelled DAT loss, not “generic low dopamine”

Use the existing **fumin expansion (Vmax multiplier zero in all compartments)** against matched wild type. It has a precise implementation and direct fly genetic, arousal and adult clearance evidence [1,10]. Cirl/Nf1 and most candidate-gene changes have no justified parameter translation. A broad DA-synthesis decrease is implementable, but is not interchangeable with transporter loss or every human ADHD mechanism.

Keep the male anatomy, exposure matrix, receptor densities, background and release calibration fixed between arms. Do not recalibrate release to bring the mutant back to wild-type dopamine; that would remove the manipulation. Initialization and pre-exposure must be specified for each condition, rather than accidentally treating different pool resets as the experimental result. Report realised source firing, chemistry and receptor terms—not only nominal external drive.

### Primary neural readout: the history effect on relative representation

Retain item 146's primary **history contrast**, not total ER firing and not decoder accuracy alone. In plain terms: **does previously presenting A rather than B change the response to the identical final pair?** Let μ be the per-cell ER response vector in the prespecified test window. With the same prefix–gap–test schedule:

```
H_vector = [μ(A prefix, A+B) − μ(A prefix, off)]
         − [μ(B prefix, A+B) − μ(B prefix, off)]
H = projection of H_vector onto an independently calibrated A-versus-B axis
raw genotype contrast = H_fumin − H_wild_type
primary contrast = H_fumin − H_predicted_from_wild_type_by_single-input_affine_fit
```

This is item 146's readout and rate-control logic applied to the genotype question, **a recommendation requiring a new freeze**, not an already authorized genotype run. Fit a nonnegative per-cell scale and an unrestricted offset using independent single-input/off controls, excluding pair outcomes; propagate the calibration uncertainty. The prediction transforms the wild-type response vectors before projection. A raw H difference alone could follow from ordinary firing-rate scaling, so report it beside, not instead of, the primary residual. Templates must come from independent male single-input data; pair outcomes must not train the axis. Report their units/normalization, distinguishability and unexplained pair-response residual. Do not force A/B coefficients to sum to one. Keep raw and time-resolved responses: equal averages can conceal alternating dominance. Correcting for additive prefix carry-over does not isolate competition-specific memory; history-matched single-input conditions would be required for that stronger claim (item 146).

Why this endpoint: it connects to measured TuBu/ring history-dependent suppression [8, A], and asks something more specific than “did dopamine increase spikes?” It is still a **candidate neural correlate relevant to selection**, not an attention test. There is no established predicted sign for fumin on this readout in the sources checked. A changed H is not automatically a deficit; unchanged H with imprecise estimates is not equivalence.

### Primary intervention: 3-IY-like reduction of effective release

Use a **predeclared reduction of effective dopamine release**, justified as a synthesis-inhibitor-like intervention by the adult ex vivo result [10]. The implementation is either an explicit `ScaleRelease` perturbation or the existing 3-IY release equation, with the **effective release fraction** as the primary axis. Specify which path is used; a direct multiplier is not an administered drug dose. This report does not choose a food dose or a neural-outcome-tuned strength. The next frozen plan must choose the strength, exposure and timing before genotype/drug neural results. Neither the existing 0.5 convention nor the adult bath concentration supplies validated male in vivo potency.

At minimum, compare wild type and fumin under vehicle and the same release-reducing intervention. The wild-type intervention arm distinguishes a genotype-specific movement toward control from a general suppression of both conditions. Freeze how “toward control” will be evaluated on H and its uncertainty; do not choose the favorable time bin, subtype or intervention strength afterward. A joint ER threshold-and-gain replay from the matched vehicle trajectory, if included, tests dependence on those implemented terms with feedback live—not anatomical exclusivity or a mediated percentage.

**Why not MPH first?** In the complete transporter knockout, the only encoded MPH action changes the denominator of a clearance term whose numerator is zero. That is an implementation-null control, not a drug-rescue experiment. Partial DAT loss plus MPH would retain a target, but the literature does not supply a justified RNAi-to-Vmax fraction or a predicted benefit on H. ATX and amphetamine require mechanisms the model does not have. Thus 3-IY-like release reduction is the narrower, runnable mechanistic question, **not a recommendation to treat human ADHD with 3-IY**.

### How stage 5 fits—and what has not transferred

- **Keep its scientific logic:** one independently chosen pair, single-input/off controls, controlled prefix history, no state reset during prefix/gap/test, matched external event realizations, actual TuBu outputs, receptor trajectories and an input-side audit. `VisualEncoder.rates` **caps the sum** of stripe drives (`behaviour/encoder.py:110–128`); A+B must not be assumed to equal A plus B at the injected cells.
- **Establish the male representation first.** FlyWire stage 4 decoded the four imposed positions and detected a non-affine change under CX_DAN activation (item 151). It establishes neither a male position code nor a fumin effect. The old stage-4 endpoint explicitly expects **228 cells** (`scripts/ring_dopamine_stage4.py:395–398`); even its analysis cannot be reused unchanged for the male trace set. Pair selection and experimental-driver/subtype correspondence must be male-specific.
- **Do not silently enlarge item 146.** Its original 24-arm scheme compares dark, CX_DAN activation and a clamp; it does not itself test ADHD genotype or drug rescue. The recommended genotype/intervention comparison needs a revised brief and frozen analysis. Keep CX_DAN drive fixed at a declared operating condition for that comparison; call any activation what it is, since fast transmission changes too. This research does not select a drive to create the desired receptor branch or neural effect.
- **Carry forward the actual earlier evidence.** Stage 3's high-inhibition ER-count change was +64.8 spikes/seed, nominal p=.036, **Holm-adjusted p=.071** for two drug/vehicle comparisons; it was not a multiplicity-controlled drug benefit. Its joint-clamp contrast was +93.4 spikes/seed with paired-t interval [+40.2,+146.6]; the remaining difference from vehicle was unresolved (items 143/145). These were female-brain fixed-input results, not attention, therapeutic rescue or a male operating-point calibration.

### What a publishable result could say

A supported strongest claim would be:

> In a fixed MaleCNS-based spiking model, removing dopamine uptake changed history-dependent representation of competing artificial inputs. Reducing effective dopamine release shifted the prespecified neural readout toward the control condition [if observed], under the stated receptor and kinetic assumptions.

If the affine-controlled primary contrast supports it, add that the change exceeded the prediction from single-input rate scaling and offset. If a clamp supports it, add that the change depended on the **implemented ER threshold/input-gain coupling**. If there is no resolved effect, report the estimate, uncertainty and the measured location of the limitation—input coding, DA separation, applied modulation or neural response. No new firing floor or success threshold is needed.

It could **not** establish ADHD in a fly; a deficit or treatment benefit in a behaving animal; visual attention, sleep or impulsivity; an effective human or fly drug dose; a causal Cirl/NF1 pathway; developmental disease; or a general result about male-versus-female brains. The connectome is one anatomical specimen, and seeds quantify stochastic variation conditional on this model, not biological replication or parameter uncertainty.

For the next experiment brief, freeze **before outcomes**: the adopted male substrate ID and all input/code hashes; anatomy-defined populations; stimulus assignments and timing; intervention strength and initial conditions; one primary contrast/window and multiplicity treatment; seed streams and seed-level uncertainty method; and a command that recreates each reported result from pinned inputs. Keep the chemical-to-neural chain, individual-seed traces and negative/unresolved outcomes. The 3D view should display the actual male cells and measured responses, labelled “artificial TuBu input,” not animate an unmeasured behavioral rescue. These are methodology recommendations, not biological pass lines.
